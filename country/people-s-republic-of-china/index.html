Blocked in People's Republic of China

The tracker lists 37 complete bans and 2 partial restrictions in People's Republic of China.

People's Republic of China's "Great Firewall" blocks most major foreign platforms by default. State-monitored domestic alternatives take their place, and unsanctioned VPN use is illegal.

Al Jazeera

Complete ban Since: Longstanding Reported Wikipedia

BBC News

Complete ban Since: 2021 Reported CNN

The BBC website has been blocked in People's Republic of China for years. In 2021, the Chinese government formally banned BBC World News television channel.

DuckDuckGo

Complete ban Since: September 2014 Reported Comparitech

Blocked by the Great Firewall since early September 2014, and still unreachable from the mainland. The authorities have never said why it was blocked. It remains accessible from the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macau, which sit outside the Great Firewall.

Complete ban Since: 2010 Reported Freedom House

Grindr

Complete ban Since: January 2022 In force Grindr support

Grindr was removed from Apple's App Store and Android app stores in People's Republic of China in January 2022. Grindr's official Help Center lists People's Republic of China as a country where the app is delisted.

Instagram

Complete ban Since: September 2014 Reported Freedom House

KakaoTalk

Complete ban Since: 2014 Enforced Citizen Lab

Blocked in People's Republic of China since July 2014 following a government crackdown on instant messaging apps, citing terrorism concerns. The Citizen Lab reported that the block was primarily implemented via DNS tampering and HTTP request filtering.

LINE

Complete ban Since: July 2014 Enforced Citizen Lab

Citizen Lab measured Great Firewall DNS tampering and HTTP filtering of LINE domains from mainland People's Republic of China, disrupting chat, contact, account and in-app-store functions. LINE said its messaging service was inaccessible in mainland People's Republic of China; current access requires a circumvention route.

LinkedIn

Complete ban Since: October 2021 Reported Freedom House

Medium

Complete ban Since: 2016 Reported Wikipedia

Blocked by the Great Firewall for hosting dissenting views and unfiltered information.

New York Times

Complete ban Since: October 2012 Reported The Guardian

The Chinese government blocked the NYT website following an investigative report into the wealth of the family of then-Premier Wen Jiabao. Later, in 2016, Apple removed the NYT news apps from its People's Republic of China App Store for "violation of local regulations."

Spotify

Complete ban Since: Never launched In force Spotify support

Spotify has never opened a mainland People's Republic of China service and does not list the territory among its supported markets. The site is also unreachable from Chinese networks rather than merely absent: OONI recorded anomalies in about 96% of measurements of spotify.com from People's Republic of China.

TikTok

Complete ban Since: 2017 Enforced Citizen Lab

ByteDance launched Douyin for the Chinese market in 2016 and released TikTok as a separate international version in 2017. TikTok is blocked in mainland People's Republic of China by the Great Firewall; only Douyin is available domestically. The two apps share core short-video technology but run on separate servers with independent content libraries, algorithms and moderation policies.

Viber's messaging and calling service was blocked on mainland Chinese networks during the 2014 crackdown on foreign chat apps; it was reachable only through a VPN in contemporaneous testing.

GitHub

Partial restriction Since: 2013 Reported Wikipedia

Subject to intermittent DNS poisoning and blocks. GitHub Gist is permanently blocked, while the main site is often heavily throttled.

Zoom

Partial restriction Since: 2020 Reported Wikipedia

Not officially banned but heavily restricted by the Great Firewall; joining meetings is possible but hosting is often unreliable, connections frequently drop, and most users rely on a VPN for consistent access. Zoom suspended direct sales and new individual signups in mainland People's Republic of China in August 2020 but still operates in the country through licensed local partners — Bizconf, Suirui Zhumu and Systec Umeet — which handle sales, support and compliance on its behalf.
